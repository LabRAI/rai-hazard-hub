Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: research website destination image, landscape 1536 x 1024.

Create an original, beautifully art-directed editorial still life for a collection
of readable wildfire research articles. The main subject is ONE open contemporary
research journal, photographed from a three-quarter overhead angle approximately
35 degrees from vertical, on a cool near-white seamless surface. Give the journal
convincing thin matte paper, a subtle central fold, and soft contact shadows.

The spread must immediately read as a publication to browse: a generous landscape
photograph of forested terrain and a small wildfire plume on the left page; a
restrained article layout on the right page with two narrow text columns, one
small red section marker, and one carefully integrated scientific map figure.
Only the short printed word "RESEARCH" needs to be readable; body text should
recede naturally at this scale. No equations, mathematics, network diagrams,
notebook, laptop, pen, fabricated numerical results, or piles of loose documents.

Let the terrain image have photographic depth and tactile detail, while remaining
printed inside the page. It must feel like a considered science publication,
with a clear relationship between image and text. It must not look like a website
screenshot pasted onto a sheet or a generic analytics dashboard.

Shared series treatment: diffuse daylight from the upper left; realistic matte
materials; cool near-white and graphite; natural forest green; subtle mineral
blue; one small vermilion accent. Rich but restrained detail. The open journal is
the unmistakable focal object and occupies most of the upper three quarters.
Keep all essential details within the central 70 percent of the width. Leave
the bottom quarter visually quiet for HTML typography. No outer frame, rounded
card, backdrop grid, neon, glossy plastic, shield, branding, headline, watermark,
or extra decorative objects. The result should be elegant and immediately
legible at 400 pixels wide, like the first image of a coordinated museum-quality
scientific editorial series.
